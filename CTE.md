# Spectre CTE Benchmarks

These benchmarks compare Spectre's (v0.0.17) compile-time evaluation (`pure` / `@static`
and implicit folding) against equivalent C, C++, Zig and Goose programs.

All five programs are short, CPU-bound kernels. Every variant prints the same
checksum; the checksums were verified across all five languages before timing.

## System

- CPU: AMD Ryzen 5 5600X-class, 6 cores / 12 threads (detected as AMD Ryzen 5 5600), 3.5–4.4 GHz
- L3 cache: 32 MiB
- RAM: 32 GB (31 GiB usable)
- OS: Linux 6.14.0-37-generic (Ubuntu 24.04)
- Spectre: self-hosted `spectre-dev`, UQBE backend, `-release -native`
- C: GCC 13.3.0, `-O3 -march=native`
- C++: GCC 13.3.0, `-O3 -march=native -std=c++20`
- Zig: 0.17.0, `ReleaseFast`, `-mcpu=native`
- Goose: runtime `4d3da949e563`, `goose build -O2`
- Harness: hyperfine 1.18.0, 3 warmups, 10 runs, `--shell=none`

Spectre's table benchmark was compiled with `-static-fuel:300000000` so the
full compile-time table generation fits in the evaluation budget.

## Results

Mean wall-clock times. Lower is better. “vs Spectre” is the speedup of Spectre
over that implementation.

| Benchmark | Spectre | C | C++ | Zig | Goose |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `fold_const` | **5.0 ms** | 5.3 ms (1.05×) | 5.2 ms (1.05×) | 5.1 ms (1.03×) | 5.4 ms (1.08×) |
| `fold_expr` | **7.2 ms** | 7.4 ms (1.03×) | 7.6 ms (1.04×) | 7.4 ms (1.02×) | 7.6 ms (1.06×) |
| `fold_loop` | **182 µs** | 493 µs (2.70×) | 489 µs (2.69×) | 415 µs (2.28×) | 635 µs (3.49×) |
| `fold_recursive` | **4.9 ms** | 5.2 ms (1.05×) | 5.2 ms (1.05×) | 5.0 ms (1.02×) | 5.2 ms (1.07×) |
| `table_hash` | **4.2 ms** | 5.0 ms (1.17×) | 4.5 ms (1.07×) | 4.4 ms (1.03×) | 5.7 ms (1.34×) |

Spectre is fastest in all five benchmarks. The largest gains are in
`fold_loop`, where the entire loop body of a pure function is evaluated once
by the compiler, and in `table_hash`, where Spectre's `@static` table is
embedded directly in the binary.

## Benchmarks

### `fold_const`

A hot dependent loop adds a compile-time `mix(seed)` result and a
`collatz(seed)` step count.

- Spectre: both pure calls are folded with constant arguments.
- C and Goose: the two resulting constants are written directly in the source,
  since neither has a comparable general-purpose CTE facility here.
- C++: `constexpr mix` / `constexpr collatz`.
- Zig: `comptime`/container-level `const` evaluation.

### `fold_expr`

A pure arithmetic `step(a, b)` is called with constant arguments in a hot loop.

- Spectre: `step(SEED_A, SEED_B)` is folded.
- C and Goose: the precomputed result is a constant.
- C++: `constexpr step`.
- Zig: comptime `step`.

### `fold_loop`

A pure `sum_to(50000)` loop is evaluated at compile time.

- Spectre: `sum_to(N)` is folded to a constant.
- C and Goose: constant `299995`.
- C++: `constexpr sum_to`.
- Zig: comptime `sum_to`.

This is the clearest demonstration of Spectre CTE: an entire runtime loop
disappears from the generated program.

### `fold_recursive`

A recursive pure `fib(24)` is evaluated at compile time and used in a hot loop.

- Spectre: `@static(fib(24))`.
- C and Goose: constant `46368`.
- C++: `constexpr fib`.
- Zig: comptime `fib` with an increased branch quota.

### `table_hash`

A 4096-entry `u64` table is generated by a 128-round xorshift mix per element,
then hashed repeatedly at runtime.

- Spectre: `@static(make_table())` generates the table at compile time and
  embeds it in the binary.
- C++: `constexpr std::array` table.
- Zig: comptime `[4096]u64` table.
- C and Goose: the equivalent table is generated at startup, because they do
  not expose a comparable general-purpose compile-time evaluation facility in
  this benchmark.
